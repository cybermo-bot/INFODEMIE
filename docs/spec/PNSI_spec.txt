01 / MINISTRY BRIEF
Executive brief / Résumé exécutif
La PNSI est une plateforme de veille sanitaire numérique et d’aide à la décision proposée pour l’ONMNE
et le Ministère de la Santé, en lien avec les partenaires du projet. Elle observe les conversations
publiques accessibles, identifie les questions et allégations sanitaires, recherche des preuves et prépare
des réponses soumises à validation humaine.
Un service national, une couverture explicitement mesurée
La première version couvre des sources sélectionnées et autorisées. Elle ne prétend pas représenter
toute la population tunisienne ni couvrir Facebook, Instagram ou TikTok sans accès confirmé. Les
langues requises sont le français, l’anglais, l’arabe standard et le dialecte tunisien, écrit en caractères
arabes ou en Arabizi. L’italien, mentionné dans la note conceptuelle pour la diaspora, reste une
extension prévue.
Les capacités attendues
Collecte et recherche multilingues; regroupement des allégations; bibliothèque de preuves approuvées;
analyse des préoccupations et de la propagation observée; tableaux de bord; alertes; rédaction de
brouillons; validation scientifique et institutionnelle; journal des actions; suivi à 48 heures, 7 jours et 30
jours.
Une réalisation professionnelle et réaliste
Interface web dédiée avec frontend et backend séparés; prise en charge complète de l’arabe de droite à
gauche; droits par institution; audit; hébergement de production en Tunisie selon le cadre validé. Les
données de démonstration sont clairement signalées. Les clés API sont fournies par la porteuse du projet
et ne sont jamais incluses dans le code ou ce document.
Ce dossier constitue un cahier de conception et de réalisation. Il n’atteste ni d’un déploiement
opérationnel, ni de la conformité juridique, ni d’un partenariat officiellement approuvé. La validation des
experts, les autorisations d’accès et les essais locaux conditionnent le passage en production.

02 / SCOPE
Scope, decisions and source of
requirements
The source document is “NOTE CONCEPTUELLE (2) (1).docx”, a 17-page PNSI proposal supplied by the
user. It specifies six functional layers, expert-reviewed responses, local hosting, differentiated
institutional access, reporting, training, outsourced operations and a national pilot. This specification
translates those ambitions into buildable requirements.
Confirmed user decisions
Implementation consequence
All health topics, not medicines only
Use an extensible taxonomy; launch with partner-selected
priorities.
French, English, Arabic and Tunisian dialect
Localize the interface; process Arabic-script Derja and
Latin-script Arabizi, including mixed posts.
No Streamlit product interface
Dedicated web application and API with persistent
workflows.
No automatic public responses
Human-approved response versions; manual publication
and recording by default.
Minimal API budget; keys supplied later
RSS/web/import baseline; optional connectors disabled

Requirement language
MUST indicates a release requirement. SHOULD is a recommended capability that may be deferred
with a documented reason. Proposed targets, scores, polling intervals and resource sizes are design
assumptions, not measurements or institutional decisions.
Not in the initial scope: unrestricted access to private groups or messaging, individual diagnosis,
inference of a person’s health status, automatic sanctions, claims of national prevalence, or causal
attribution of health outcomes to communication alone. Broad platform coverage, Italian, large-scale
audiovisual processing and epidemiological integration are gated extensions.

Scope and accountability
Every case, assessment, response, action and report belongs to an institution or a deliberately shared
workspace. Shared access is explicit and revocable. Backend authorization applies to objects, search
results, aggregates, exports and attachments; a hidden menu is not a security boundary.
Assign each case an owner, reviewer, response editor, due date and escalation contact. Store transitions
in an append-only audit trail. Keep ONMNE operational oversight and Ministry decision views from the
note; confirm whether the proposed outsourced operating model actually applies before assigning
contracts or responsibilities.

04 / WORKFLOW
End-to-end operational workflow
Stage
Input, output and mandatory gate
1. Configure
Approved source record, topic terms, access route and
collection schedule.
2. Collect
Permitted public material -> provenance record; record
failures and watermarks.
3. Prepare
Redact personal data, detect language spans, normalize a
copy, deduplicate.
4. Understand
Identify assertions, questions, reported speech and
candidate claims.
5. Group and triage
Link related mentions; analyst confirms case boundaries
and priority.
6. Retrieve evidence
Search approved documents; store passages, versions and
relevance.
7. Assess
AI proposes status; scientific reviewer resolves or marks
uncertainty.
8. Draft and approve
Evidence-grounded draft -> language review ->
institutional approval.
9. Record action
Responsible person publishes externally; record channel,
time and exact version.
10. Follow up
Observe changes at 48h, 7d, 30d; document limits and
lessons.
A case may be closed as a question, legitimate criticism, duplicate or irrelevant content without a
corrective response. A high-harm, unverified claim can warrant urgent investigation before enough
evidence exists for a conclusion. Priority and evidence status are independent.
Each stage must expose its status, last successful operation, owner and failure reason. Retries resume
from the last safe checkpoint. Evidence updates may reopen an assessment; an edited response loses
approval. Publication remains a human action in the initial release.

07 / INGESTION
Source registry and connector
contracts
Maintain separate monitoring_sources and evidence_sources. A publisher may appear in both only
after two independent approvals with different scopes. Each monitoring record MUST contain ID,
institution, name, exact host/feed, platform, permitted method, permission reference, allowed fields,
languages, topics, schedule, quota, retention policy, owner, last check and enabled state.
Connector interface
Implement validate_config(), healthcheck(), fetch_batch(cursor, since, limit), normalize(item)
and apply_deletions(). Return records, next cursor, watermark, rate-limit information and partial
errors. Required item fields: source ID, platform item ID or permitted hash, published/fetched UTC
timestamps, text, language hints, available engagement counts, content type, access status and
provenance visibility.
Use idempotency on source plus item identity; update edits as versions instead of new independent
posts. Retry transient errors with exponential backoff and jitter; respect Retry-After. Quarantine
repeated schema failures and route dead-letter jobs to an operator. A missing key yields “not
configured”; a denied request yields “access denied”; neither yields invented data.
Collection policy
Proposed pilot: RSS every 30 minutes; permitted website lists every 2-6 hours; API polling according to
quotas. Values are configurable and measured in the pilot. Sanitize HTML and retain relevant headings.
No access to private groups. Author-submitted exports require rights and redaction checks. Preserve
original and normalized text separately; apply source deletion rules to cached text, vectors and derived
examples.

09 / EVIDENCE PIPELINE
Evidence governance and document
ingestion
Evidence source lifecycle: proposed -> reviewed -> approved -> suspended/retired. A source
steward proposes changes; a scientific reviewer approves scope and authoritative document types.
Every change records actor, date, reason and version. Emergency source additions still require
documented review.
Record
Required fields
Evidence source
Organization, exact host/path scopes, topic remit,
jurisdiction, allowed document types, approval state,
permissions, reviewer, review date.
Document
Title, canonical URL, issuing body, language,
publication/effective/review date, version, checksum,
retrieval time, permission, withdrawn/superseded flags.
Passage
Document/version ID, page/section/offset, text, extraction
method and confidence, topic, embedding version.
Claim-evidence link
Supporting/contradicting/contextual relation, relevance
explanation, population fit, date fit, reviewer status.
Processing pipeline
Fetch approved resources or accept authorized uploads; detect MIME type; scan uploads; extract text,
with OCR only when required; preserve pages and headings; redact any accidental personal data; create
semantically coherent passages; index keyword and multilingual-vector representations; validate
sampled extraction against the original. The reader must be able to open the relevant page or passage.
Use provisional 400-700-token chunks with modest overlap; evaluate retrieval before settling sizes. Poll
urgent alerts more often than stable guidelines. Track supersession explicitly. A document without a
date is not automatically current. If a critical source is unavailable, display the failure and allow
authorized manual upload; never manufacture a quote or cite an unseen full text.

10 / VERIFICATION
Claim extraction, verification and
abstention
Identify atomic, checkable claims while preserving context, negation, timing, population, quantity and
source stance. Separate author assertions, questions, sarcasm, quotations and reported rumors. A post
can contain several claims. Merge only equivalent claims; “can cause” and “always causes” must not be
treated as the same statement.
Verification sequence
Create a neutral claim representation in the original language and a retrieval translation. Search the
approved local corpus first, then approved international sources and NCBI discovery where appropriate.
Retrieve both supporting and contradicting material. Rerank by meaning, authority, currentness and
local applicability. Produce a structured assessment linking each conclusion to passages. A scientific
reviewer confirms or changes it.
Evidence status
Meaning
Unassessed
No substantive verification completed.
Supported
Evidence supports the claim in the stated context.
Contradicted
Relevant evidence contradicts it.
Misleading / missing context
Some factual content but an unjustified interpretation,
omission or generalization.
Insufficient evidence
Not enough reliable evidence to decide.
Conflicting evidence
Credible relevant sources disagree; specialist resolution
needed.
Not a factual claim
Question, opinion or criticism requiring a different
response.
“Rumor” describes circulation of an unverified claim, not a false verdict. “Disinformation” implies intent;
do not infer intent from text classification alone. Critical disagreement with institutions is not evidence of
falsehood. Keep machine proposals distinct from reviewer-confirmed assessments and provide a
correction/reopening mechanism.

11 / ANALYTICS
Scores, indicators and alert
thresholds
Maintain three separate values: priority for action, evidence status for the claim, and model
confidence only when calibrated. Do not display an invented “truth percentage”. A model’s
self-reported certainty is not a measured probability.
Proposed triage score, subject to validation
Priority = 100 × (0.45H + 0.25G + 0.15E + 0.15V), where H is expert-assessed potential health harm, G
is growth within observed source coverage, E is available engagement normalized within platform, and V
is exposure proxy based on collected mentions. Components are scaled 0-1, with explanations. These
weights are initial design choices, not WHO-endorsed values.
When data are missing, show score completeness and a provisional/manual priority rather than silently
treating unknowns as zero. Do not compare like counts directly across platforms. Use stable-source
baseline windows and mark API outages or source additions. Never call a collected-post count “people
reached” without a valid reach measure.
Proposed alert rule
Guard / action
Expert flags immediate potential harm
Escalate even with low volume; evidence may remain
uncertain.
At least 10 deduplicated mentions and >=3x baseline
growth
Analyst review; a coverage change must not be mistaken
for a surge.
Priority >=70; >=40 medium
Pilot thresholds only; tune against reviewed cases and
capacity.
Repeated signal for same case
Deduplicate, apply cooldown; reopen for meaningful
changes.
The concept note’s “infodemic temperature” should remain an experimental composite with visible
components, denominator and uncertainty. Never present it as an established national metric. Alerts
need an owner, rationale, due time, acknowledgement and escalation history.

Layer
Mandatory behavior
UI locales

Tunisian Derja interface strings. Language selection
persists per user.
Content handling
Arabic-script Derja, Arabizi, French, English, standard
Arabic and mixed-language spans. Unknown language is

Response variants
French, English, Arabic and Derja, with separate
Arabic-script and Arabizi variants where requested;

Arabic/Arabic-script Derja mirror layout; Latin Arabizi

Future extension

Keep original text unchanged; store normalization and translation as derived views. Arabizi digits can

Search and rendering
Use language-aware keyword expansion plus evaluated multilingual embeddings. Query in one language
should retrieve equivalent claims in another; expose original and translation side by side. RTL tables,
forms, charts, drawers, pagination and PDFs require testing. Dates and number formats follow locale;
timestamps remain UTC internally and display with an explicit timezone.
Accessibility target: WCAG 2.2 AA design practices, verified in testing. Keyboard navigation, visible
focus, chart table alternatives, readable contrast, scalable text, descriptive labels and status words
alongside colors are required. Derja medical wording and translated corrections must be reviewed
locally; translation alone does not establish scientific equivalence.

13 / ENGINEERING
Architecture and technology choices
Recommended baseline: a modular backend with asynchronous workers, rather than many
independent microservices before the pilot. This preserves clear domain boundaries without imposing
Kafka, Kubernetes, Neo4j or several databases from day one. Split services only after measured scale or
isolation requirements justify it.
Component
Proposed implementation
Web frontend
Next.js, React, TypeScript; accessible component system,
Tailwind styling, localized routing [R13].
API
FastAPI with typed request/response models and
generated OpenAPI [R14]; domain services and centralized
authorization.
Persistent data
PostgreSQL for cases, roles, workflows and audit; pgvector
as an optional evaluated vector extension.
Files
Locally hosted S3-compatible object storage; private
signed access, malware scanning and retention jobs.
Workers
Celery + Redis, with a scheduler, persistent job records
and idempotency; separate CPU/optional GPU queues.
Models
Provider adapters for local or approved external inference;
separate extraction, retrieval/reranking and drafting roles.
Identity
Institutional OIDC when available; secure local accounts for
isolated development only.
Operations
Containerized services, reverse proxy/TLS, health checks,
metrics, logs, backups and deployment runbook.
Data path: browser -> API -> PostgreSQL/private storage; API -> queue -> workers -> permitted sources
or approved model adapters; workers -> evidence/search indexes -> API. The browser never calls a
secret-bearing social or model API directly.
Keep collection, evidence, inference, review, action and reporting modules separate. Adopt maintained
stable dependency versions at implementation time, lock them and scan licenses/vulnerabilities. No
version number here is a promise that a package will remain supported. These are engineering
proposals, not a prescribed ministry stack.

Mutation requests include expected_version and, for retry-sensitive operations, Idempotency-Key. Errors
use code, safe message, field errors and request_id; never expose secrets. Return 401/403 for
authorization, 409 for version conflicts, 422 for validation, 429 with retry guidance and 503 for
unavailable dependencies. Authorization checks occur before counts or file access.
Background job progress uses polling first, optional server-sent events later. Exports are scoped,

20 / RESPONSE WORKFLOW
Response generation and human
sign-off
Input to drafting: reviewer-confirmed claim assessment, approved evidence passages, intended
audience, language/script, channel and approved institutional communication guidance. If evidence is
unresolved, draft an uncertainty-aware explanation or investigation notice instead of asserting a
correction.
Draft structure
Lead with the accurate information in simple language; acknowledge the underlying concern; explain
the relevant evidence and limits; provide a practical next step agreed by reviewers; include references
and a checked date. Avoid sensational repetition of a harmful claim, stigmatization, personal medical
instructions or fabricated official attribution. Channel adaptations can include posts, FAQs, spokesperson
Q&A; and short video scripts.
Approval state machine
Draft -> scientific_review -> language_review -> institutional_review -> approved ->
action_recorded -> follow_up -> closed. Rejection returns to changes_requested with a reason.
Individual variants can have different states; approval of French does not approve Arabic or Arabizi. Only
reviewed current evidence is eligible for an approved factual correction.
Approval binds response ID, immutable version, language, script, cited assessment version, evidence
snapshot and approver. Editing any factual wording, translation, citations or assessment invalidates the
affected approval. The backend enforces this even if a client directly calls the approve endpoint.
No automatic external publishing in the baseline. Copy/export the approved variant, have the authorized
person share it, then record what actually occurred. Optional publishing integrations require explicit
institutional scope, platform access and a separate evaluated implementation. AI-produced text remains
clearly labeled until reviewed.

23 / DATA GOVERNANCE
Privacy, data residency and the
actor-map conflict
The concept note requires no retained personal identifiers, while also requesting maps of accounts,
influencers and propagation networks. These are not automatically compatible. Pseudonymized IDs and
searchable text can still allow identification. Removing a username does not establish anonymity.
Baseline design choice
Use aggregate narrative/source maps and organization-level publisher information where approved. Do
not build individual influencer profiles or personal health inference in the initial release. Redact names,
handles, phone numbers, email addresses and sensitive identifiers from working examples; minimize
original text storage and review residual identification risk.
Two provenance options require institutional resolution: (A) strict minimization, retaining
source/document identity without personal post links; or (B) a restricted provenance vault for authorized
traceability, with defined lawful basis, retention and access audit. Option B departs from a literal “no
identifiers retained” policy and cannot be enabled merely for convenience. Use A as the default until this
is resolved.
Residency and retention
Production data hosting in Tunisia is a requirement in the note. External model, OCR, logging or email
services may transfer data even if the database is local. Use local processing by default for sensitive
material; external processing requires a recorded institutional decision. This document does not certify
legal compliance.
Proposed starting retention: raw ingestion buffers <=24h; redacted working items <=90 days; evidence
according to rights/currentness; action/audit history according to institutional policy. These values are
provisional. Deletions must cover storage, caches, embeddings, examples and recoverable backups
according to the approved policy. WHO ethics guidance is a relevant framework [R1], not a substitute for
local review.

25 / AI
AI implementation and
model-selection strategy
Use separate tasks with typed outputs: health-topic relevance, language spans, assertion/question
stance, atomic claim extraction, semantic matching, narrative clustering, evidence retrieval/reranking,
assessment proposal and grounded response drafting. Start with evaluated pretrained components; do
not assume custom training is necessary for every task.
Benchmark before selection
Candidate local models must be tested on Tunisian Derja, Arabizi, Arabic, French and English. Compare
against a simple keyword/lexical baseline and an approved hosted model only if policy permits. Record
license, hardware needs, per-task quality, abstention, latency, inference cost and language errors. No
named model is declared best without this benchmark.
RAG flow: approved corpus -> versioned passages -> multilingual keyword/vector retrieval -> reranking
-> evidence-fit checks -> constrained assessment with passage IDs -> expert review. Embedding
similarity finds related text; it does not verify a claim. A language model should not act as the sole
source of truth.
Structured output and failure modes
Assessment output includes claim ID, proposed status, rationale, supporting/contradicting passage IDs,
missing evidence, context limitations and model-run ID. Validate JSON with typed schemas and bounded
retries; unresolved parse/grounding failures produce “review required”. Set generation budgets and
maximum evidence context sizes.
Keep prompt templates versioned in code, with controlled release changes. Run model regression
evaluations when prompts, corpus, embeddings or model weights change. Never train on collected
platform data unless permission allows it. Reviewer decisions become a versioned annotation dataset
only under the approved data-handling plan.

Appendix B: permitted image and
audio processing
Audiovisual capability is part of the national ambition, but API metadata access does not automatically
grant permission to download videos or audio. Enable processing only for authorized media from allowed
sources or institutional uploads. Prioritize relevant items rather than processing every file.
Processing steps
Validate type/size and scan; place in private temporary storage; extract permitted audio; detect
language segments; transcribe speech with timestamps and uncertainty; sample relevant frames; run
OCR on text regions; combine aligned text without inventing unseen content; redact identifiers; link
derived claims to exact time/frame/page; delete temporary media according to rights and retention.
Separate component evaluation
Compare transcription and OCR candidates on Tunisian speech, noise, accents, code-switching and
low-resolution overlays. Measure word/character errors plus medically important omissions: negation,
numbers, units, product names and the speaker’s stance. Good overall transcription accuracy can still
miss a critical “not”.
Let analysts correct transcripts and inspect permitted time segments. Store the original derived
transcript, corrected version, extraction model/version and confidence limits. Low-quality material is
queued for manual review; missing audio or unreadable text yields unavailable/uncertain rather than an
invented transcript.
Keep OCR/ASR as optional asynchronous worker modules with independent resource budgets. A
text-first pilot is fully usable without them. Do not label a video verified solely from its title, thumbnail or
comments; make the scope of the actual reviewed material explicit.
